Floor plan

Drawn from the measurements taken on 9 September. Scroll sideways on a phone, or pinch to zoom.

How to read it

The room is 415 cm between wall faces, not 450. Your 450 on the internal wall runs 52 to the house door, 98 across it, then 300 to the garden door itself. That last 300 passes the back wall face at 415, carries on 22 through the reveal to the door frame, and about 13 more to the door leaf. Both long walls agree once the reveal is accounted for: 155 + 21 + 183 + 21 + 35 on the outside wall also makes 415.

Only two walls get battened. The front wall and the back wall are already plastered and just need their bases patched. The outside wall gets 47×100 battens on edge so its new face lands flush with the two piers, which project 11 cm. The internal wall gets 47×75. The internal wall stays thin for the last 20 cm before the back wall and through the reveal, with plasterboard bonded straight to the block, so the garden door can still swing to about 83°. If you want the full 90°, the thin strip would need to run about 60 cm instead of 20.

Joists run across the 2.5 m width so each sits on a level line of the sloping slab. Eleven of them at 400 mm centres; the two that land on the piers are cut 11 cm shorter, and a short bearer in the door reveal carries the deck to the threshold.

The sink sits under the outside-wall window over the existing waste. The cold feed comes in on the internal wall near the garden door, so it crosses the room inside a joist bay under the floor.

Estimated rather than measured, so check on site: the depth of the recess in the back wall, the exact positions of the old pipe holes and sockets, and which of the three low holes is the live sink waste. The drawing on its own opens in any browser and prints to A4.

Trades to book

What to ask each trade for, so quotes come back comparable.

Plumbing is being done in-house; see the plumbing note under Build notes. Only two trades to book.

Electrician · two visits, budget £350 to £650 all in

Ask for: four to six twin sockets around the bench positions added to the existing supply, six fire-rated downlights on a new switch replacing the pendant, a 100 mm ceiling extractor on a timer with its own isolator and ducting to outside, and Minor Works Certificates for the additions. Mention the service gap behind the plasterboard (45 mm on the outside wall, 20 mm on the internal wall) so cables run without chasing, and that 35 mm dry-lining boxes fit. Ask whether a 16 A commando outlet for machinery is worth adding while the wall is open.

Why the range. Because the room already has power, this is additions to an existing circuit rather than a new one, which is not notifiable under Part P and needs about a day: first fix before the boards go on, second fix after the skim. At the South West day rate of £260 to £380 plus £80 to £150 of cable, boxes and fittings, expect £350 to £650, less if you supply the lights and fan from Order 3. The one thing that can push it up: if the existing circuit turns out to have no RCD protection or no spare capacity, the electrician will want a new circuit from the consumer unit, which is notifiable and puts the job at £800 to £1,400, more again if the board needs changing. Ask them to check that first.

Build notes

Floor design

Eleven 47×200 C24 treated joists (actual 45×195) at 400 mm centres run across the 2.5 m width, so each joist sits on a level contour of the sloping slab and needs one packer thickness along its whole length. The 195 mm joist provides almost all the height: you only pack 5 mm at the front-wall end rising to about 55 mm at the garden-door end. The two joists that meet the outside-wall piers are cut 110 mm shorter; a short bearer in the door reveal carries the deck to the threshold. Joist ends are tied to the block walls with light angle brackets above the DPM upstand, so nothing punctures the membrane. 100 mm PIR strips push up flush under 22 mm P5 tongue-and-groove chipboard.

Packing guide

Joist 1 is 50 mm from the front wall (the high end). Joist 11 is 100 mm from the back wall face, with the reveal bearer beyond it. Set every joist to the laser line; this table is the guide for what to reach for.

Five packing points per joist at roughly 600 mm spacing. The spare half joist and the 440 mm offcuts become the reveal bearer and noggins for the middle bays. Board ends must land on a joist: the first board in each row is cut so its far end sits on a joist centre, then the offcut starts the next row.

Wall design

Only the two long block walls are lined. On the outside wall, 47×100 treated battens (actual 45×95) fixed on edge bring the new face out to 107 mm, level with the two 110 mm piers, so the finished wall is flat and the piers vanish into it; the plasterer skims straight over the pier faces. On the internal wall, 47×75 (actual 45×70) does the same job at 82 mm. Both walls: verticals at 600 mm centres with a horizontal batten top and bottom, DPC behind every piece of timber, 50 mm PIR between the battens against the block with every joint foil-taped, and 12.5 mm tapered-edge plasterboard finishing 10 mm above the floor. The gap between PIR and board (45 mm outside, 20 mm inside) is the electrician's service void.

The garden-door corner. The door hinges on the internal-wall side and swings into the room, so a full-depth lining on the internal wall would stop it opening. The battens stop 20 cm short of the back wall face; that strip and the 22 cm reveal get plasterboard bonded straight to the block with foam adhesive. With that the door opens to about 83°, which is enough to walk through and carry timber in. If you want a full 90°, extend the thin strip to about 60 cm. The plasterer beads the step where the thick lining meets the thin one.

The front wall and back wall are already plastered and stay as they are apart from patching the bases. Inside the plaster the room finishes roughly 4.0 m × 2.3 m.

Plumbing, done yourself

Everything here is cold water and 15 mm pipe, so push-fit does the lot with no soldering. Isolate at the stopcock, open the outside tap to drain down, cut each dead leg square with a pipe slice, deburr the end, mark the insertion depth and push the fitting fully home until it clicks. Stop ends on the redundant colds and on the hot pipe behind Emma's vanity (that one may be 22 mm, so check before buying). A push-fit tee where the single cold feed splits, an isolating valve on the sink branch, then a flexible tap connector onto the tap tail. The waste is 40 mm solvent-weld or push-fit pipe from a bottle trap under the sink, falling at least 1 in 40 to the drain. Turn the water back on slowly and check every joint dry after an hour and again the next day before anything gets boarded over.

Ceiling, lights and fan

The ceiling stays. The old pendant position is patched with a plasterboard offcut on noggins and scrimmed. Six downlights in two rows of three suit the room: rows about 830 mm in from each long wall, fittings about 1.4 m apart along the room. Fire-rated IP65 fittings are the safe default under a garage roof. The extractor mounts in the ceiling towards the outside wall and ducts through the void to a louvre on that wall above ceiling level, or to the soffit; the electrician confirms the exit before cutting. A wall-mounted fan high on the outside wall does the same job with no ducting at all if the void turns out to be awkward.

The ceiling is textured Artex. Skimming over it is standard practice. Cutting seven holes in it makes dust, so if the house predates 2000 get the texture tested for asbestos first (a lab test is about £30 to £50). If it is clear, cut away; if not, the holes need a licensed approach and the plasterer should know before quoting.

Decisions and assumptions

Sink position is settled. The existing sink waste on the outside wall sits below the 60×60 window, so the sink goes there. The other old pipe holes (washing machine, tumble dryer, toilet and shower) all get filled. The cold feed crosses the room under the floor from the isolator on the internal wall.
Ceiling height assumed at 2.4 m. The garden door head is at 2.33 m so the ceiling is a little above that. Wall quantities use 2.4 m. If you measure more than 2.41 m, the boards go up horizontally in two rows with a cut strip at the top; the board count still holds.
Room length. The plan uses 415 cm between the front wall face and the back wall face, with the 450 you measured running on into the garden-door reveal. If a tape from the front wall to the back wall face reads 450, tell me and the plan and joist count change.
Cold feed height. The existing isolator stub on the internal wall is about 300 mm above the slab, which is right at new floor level. Extend it with push-fit to at least 400 mm before the floor is decked, or drop it into the floor void and run it across to the sink.
Artex ceiling. If the house was built before 2000, have the texture tested before anyone cuts the downlight and fan holes. Skimming over it is fine either way.
Insulation choice. Mineral wool would save about £158 on the floor. It is not recommended here: wool on a cold slab under a sink, in a room that has already flooded, holds water and never dries. PIR does not.
Downlights. Six assumed. Four is enough for general light; six is better for bench work. Adjust the electrician brief to taste.
