Trades to book

What to ask each trade for, so quotes come back comparable.

Plumbing is being done in-house; see the plumbing note under Build notes. Only two trades to book.

Electrician · two visits, budget £800 to £1,400 all in

Ask for: a new circuit from the consumer unit for four to six twin sockets around the bench positions, six fire-rated downlights on a new switch replacing the pendant, a 100 mm ceiling extractor on a timer with its own isolator and ducting to outside, and the Electrical Installation Certificate plus Part P notification. Mention the 20 mm service gap behind the plasterboard so cables run without chasing, and that 35 mm dry-lining boxes fit. Ask whether a 16 A or 32 A commando outlet for machinery is worth adding while the circuit is in.

Why the range. A new circuit to a garage or workshop is notifiable under Part P, so use a registered electrician (NICEIC, NAPIT or similar) who notifies Building Control and issues the compliance certificate. Individual guide prices add up to £930 to £1,800 if priced as separate jobs; as one visit of one and a half to two days at the South West day rate of £260 to £380 plus £150 to £250 of cable, boxes and an RCBO, expect £800 to £1,400. Fittings you supply come off that. If the consumer unit has no spare way, add £550 to £900 for a board change.

Build notes

Floor design

Twelve 47×200 C24 treated joists (actual 45×195) at 400 mm centres run across the 2.5 m width, so each joist sits on a level contour of the sloping slab and needs one packer thickness along its whole length. The 195 mm joist provides almost all the height: you only pack 5 mm at the high end rising to 55 mm at the garden-door end. Joist ends are tied to the block walls with light angle brackets above the DPM upstand, so nothing punctures the membrane. 100 mm PIR strips push up flush under 22 mm P5 tongue-and-groove chipboard.

Packing guide

Joist 1 is 50 mm from the end wall at the high end (small window). Joist 12 is 50 mm from the garden-door wall. Set every joist to the laser line; this table is the guide for what to reach for.

JoistsPacking under joistMade from
1 to 45 to 19 mmPlastic shims only
5 to 923 to 41 mm25×50 treated batten full length, plus shims
10 to 1246 to 55 mm47×50 treated batten full length, plus shims

Five packing points per joist at roughly 600 mm spacing. The six 440 mm joist offcuts become noggins for the middle bays. Board ends must land on a joist: the first board in each row is cut so its far end sits on a joist centre, then the offcut starts the next row.

Wall design

Treated 47×75 battens (actual 45×70) vertical at 600 mm centres with a horizontal batten top and bottom, DPC strip behind every batten so no timber touches block. 50 mm PIR sits between the battens against the block with every joint foil-taped, which leaves a 20 mm service gap behind the plasterboard for the electrician's cables and back boxes. 12.5 mm tapered-edge plasterboard finishes 10 mm above the floor. Each wall grows by about 85 mm, so the room ends up roughly 4.4 m × 2.33 m inside the plaster.

The wall by the garden door is already plastered and stays as it is. The plasterer blends the new boards into it at the corners.

Plumbing, done yourself

Everything here is cold water and 15 mm pipe, so push-fit does the lot with no soldering. Isolate at the stopcock, open the outside tap to drain down, cut each dead leg square with a pipe slice, deburr the end, mark the insertion depth and push the fitting fully home until it clicks. Stop ends on the redundant colds and on the hot pipe behind Emma's vanity (that one may be 22 mm, so check before buying). A push-fit tee where the single cold feed splits, an isolating valve on the sink branch, then a flexible tap connector onto the tap tail. The waste is 40 mm solvent-weld or push-fit pipe from a bottle trap under the sink, falling at least 1 in 40 to the drain. Turn the water back on slowly and check every joint dry after an hour and again the next day before anything gets boarded over.

Ceiling, lights and fan

The ceiling stays. The old pendant position is patched with a plasterboard offcut on noggins and scrimmed. Six downlights in two rows of three suit a 4.5 m × 2.5 m room: rows about 800 mm in from each long wall, fittings about 1.1 m apart along the room. Fire-rated IP65 fittings are the safe default under a garage roof. The extractor mounts in the ceiling and ducts through the void to the outside; the electrician needs to confirm the exit point (wall terminal on the garden-facing wall, or a tile or soffit vent) before cutting anything.

Decisions and assumptions

Sink waste route. The three holes you want to fill include the only two drains in the room (the washing machine waste and the toilet/shower waste). A sink needs a 40 mm waste to a drain. Cheapest is to keep the washing machine waste open and run the sink to it, filling only the tumble dryer vent and the toilet/shower waste. Otherwise you need to core a new hole through the block and run a new 40 mm waste to an outside gully.
Wall height assumed at 2.3 m. The wall quantities below use 2.3 m. Measure floor-to-ceiling and if it is nearer 2.5 m add one plasterboard and one PIR board.
Cold feed height. The existing isolator stub on the long wall is about 300 mm above the slab, which is right at new floor level. Extend it with push-fit to at least 400 mm before the floor is decked, or route it inside the floor void to the sink position.
Insulation choice. Mineral wool would save about £158 on the floor. It is not recommended here: wool on a cold slab under a sink, in a room that has already flooded, holds water and never dries. PIR does not.
Downlights. Six assumed. Four is enough for general light; six is better for bench work. Adjust the electrician brief to taste.
Extractor exit. Route to outside not yet known. If the ceiling void is a flat roof with no easy exit, a wall-mounted fan high on the garden-facing wall does the same job for less ducting.
